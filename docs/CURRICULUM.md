# ChessForge curriculum map

The curriculum is designed to grow from first principles to advanced competitive play. Each concept should eventually have an explanation, annotated examples, guided practice, independent assessment, and scheduled recall.

## Foundations and board vision

- Rules, legal moves, check, checkmate, stalemate, and draw rules
- Board coordinates, files, ranks, diagonals, and square colors
- Piece values as heuristics, not fixed laws
- Attacked, defended, loose, pinned, and overloaded pieces
- King safety, development, center control, and castling
- Threat scanning and blunder prevention

## Tactical vision

- Forks, double attacks, pins, skewers, and X-rays
- Discovered attacks, discovered checks, and double checks
- Deflection, decoy, interference, clearance, and overloading
- Removing the defender, attraction, obstruction, and zwischenzug
- Back-rank patterns, mating nets, smothered mate, and common mating patterns
- Tactical sacrifices, exchange sacrifices, and defensive resources

## Calculation and visualization

- Candidate-move generation and forcing-move scans
- Checks, captures, threats, and opponent-response enumeration
- Quiet moves, intermediate moves, and defensive resources
- Variation trees, stopping points, and evaluation of resulting positions
- Board visualization, blindfold reconstruction, and move-sequence recall
- Calculation discipline and time allocation

## Positional understanding and planning

- Piece activity, coordination, good and bad bishops, strong knights, and outposts
- Pawn islands, isolated/doubled/backward pawns, pawn chains, and passed pawns
- Weak squares, color complexes, files, diagonals, and space
- Pawn breaks, minority attacks, central control, and structural transformations
- Prophylaxis, restriction, initiative, exchanges, and improving the worst piece

## Attack and defense

- Attacking exposed kings, open files, piece coordination, and attacking prerequisites
- Building an initiative and converting it into a durable advantage
- Defensive resources, counterplay, exchanges, fortresses, and perpetual check
- When to accept, decline, or return material

## Openings

- Open games, semi-open games, closed games, and flank openings
- Opening principles, move orders, transpositions, and ECO classification
- Named families and variations, typical structures, plans, and tactical motifs
- Repertoire building, practical alternatives, traps, and preparation
- Opening recall and learning ideas rather than memorizing moves alone

## Middlegames and endgames

- Typical middlegame plans arising from pawn structures
- King and pawn endings, opposition, triangulation, and zugzwang
- Rook endings, rook activity, Lucena, Philidor, and defensive techniques
- Minor-piece endings, opposite-colored bishops, and practical conversion
- Queen endings, fortresses, tablebase positions, and theoretical mates

## Competitive and reflective skills

- Clock management and decision quality under time pressure
- Practical play in better, equal, and worse positions
- Tournament rules, notation, draw claims, and time controls
- Game review, critical-moment analysis, mistake classification, and training plans
- Transfer tests using positions the learner has not previously seen
